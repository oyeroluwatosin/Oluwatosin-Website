FIG. 04 — TEAM LEAD

KloFood: making food waste visible to students.

A responsive web service that helps Tampere university students track, reduce and get rewarded for cutting their own food waste. I led the UX design and research.

The problem

Waste is easy to ignore when you can’t see it.

The UNEP Food Waste Index 2021 put annual global food waste at over 931 million tonnes. It estimated Finnish households at 64–67 kg per person, with about 23–24 kg per person from food services and 26 kg from retail.

The UN goal (SDG 12.3) is to halve food waste and food loss by 2030. We proposed a web service that meets the goals of one group, Tampere university students, and works towards that target.

Research

What students actually asked for.

We began with pilot interviews with colleagues to check we were asking the right questions. Then we ran nine semi-structured interviews, which allowed planned questions as well as follow-ups based on what people said.

I ran 3 of the 9 (Nigerian and Vietnamese participants). I transcribed with Microsoft Teams and Descript, removed superfluous detail, and grouped recurring findings through thematic coding.

From research to features

Each feature answers something students said.

We built on a food-ordering platform, a pattern many students already use, with a twist aimed at reducing waste. The calorie calculator some students asked for was not part of the design.

  • Students asked for notifications of food expiry dates and preservation tips

    What we designed A Kitchen page where users sort what they ordered by where it is stored (fridge, freezer or cabinet), with reminders. Items are entered by the user on purpose.

  • Students asked for ways to use up leftovers

    What we designed A Recipe page that suggests what to do with leftovers.

  • Students asked for a simple way to see how much they save

    What we designed An Impact page with a weekly food-waste target and clear measures of money and kilograms saved.

  • Students asked for a way to contribute earnings to people who need them

    What we designed A rewards system where users earn points they can trade or donate to less privileged people.

  • Students asked for clear indication of allergies

    What we designed Clear labels for allergies and diet-related food on every item.

  • Students asked for stores and restaurants near them

    What we designed Access to multiple verified stores and restaurants, including near the university campuses.

My role

Leading the team and designing the structure.

As team lead I facilitated project planning and delivery, and used data and analytics to drive decisions for our proposed clients. I designed the sitemap, concept map and user tasks, and mapped the UX goals through user flows and prototypes.

I turned ideas into low-fidelity designs for usability testing, designed some high-fidelity pages, created the design system that guided our visual design, and planned retention ideas such as gamified rewards.

What testing taught me

Easy to use isn’t enough. It has to be easy to understand.

Usability testing exposed problems I’d overlooked: how we worded information, and weak reasons to come back. We refined the language and added retention programmes to motivate users.

Since then I validate earlier with real users, before the wording and flows harden. I also grew in listening to the team and in research methods: getting insights, grouping issues with affinity mapping, and asking open-minded questions.

What’s next

Ideas we did not get to build.

A partners section, which would need more interviews with food-service stakeholders. A donation feature so users can support verified food-aid programmes. Sharing of goals reached, and of the Your Impact page, on social media. More gamification to keep people motivated and consistent.

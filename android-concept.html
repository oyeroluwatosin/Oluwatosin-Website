Self-initiated concept · not client work

Designing the moments an Android system gets in the way.

A practice study on three things Android products do all the time and users notice straight away: asking for permission, failing gracefully, and deciding what deserves attention.

Role
UX Designer (solo)
Platform
Android · 360 dp frame
Scenario
Company-managed handheld scanner app
Tool
Figma (screens shown here as HTML)
Why I made this

I design role-based experiences. This is me stretching toward the Android system layer.

My day-to-day work is multi-role platforms: four user levels, with critical actions reserved for admins. Device-managed Android products have the same shape. An admin sets the rules, and the person holding the device lives with them.

I use Android every day and I’m studying Material Design to align this work with it. This concept covers states I haven’t shipped before: permission rationale and denial, offline, and notification priority. It is deliberately honest about being a practice piece.

The notification rules (screen 04)

My own pain point: too many notifications, cleared by hand.

I clear my notification shade by hand, often. The idea: let priority decide what stays, returns or quietly expires. I know Android already has channels, importance levels, snooze and developer-set timeouts. The gap I’m exploring is making expiry visible and controllable by the user.

Principles and what I’d validate next

Three rules I designed to.

01

Design for the denied path

Every permission needs a rationale, a “no” path and a way back. The no-path is where trust is won or lost.

02

Say what’s safe, not just what failed

Offline and error states should protect the user’s confidence: what is saved, what is pending, what happens next.

03

Priority must be visible

If the system decides what matters, the user should be able to see and change that decision.

Not yet done

This is a concept: no usability testing, no developer review, and not yet checked line by line against Material Design 3. Loading and empty states are the next screens I’d add.

How I’d validate it

Test with five Android users on the denied and offline flows, measure how often people clear notifications by hand before and after, and review feasibility with Android developers before committing to the expiry rules.
